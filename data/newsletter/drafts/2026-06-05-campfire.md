## 1) What CampIn Learned This Week

The strongest pattern in the current lead set is simple: supply exists, but proof is uneven. Water shows up as a concern across the full set, washroom across most of it, and road access still breaks trust faster than pretty photos can repair it.

For Indian campers, the trust layer is not more discovery. It is better confirmation before the car turns off the highway.

## 2) Practical Camping Answer: The 90-second overnight viability test

Before you commit to any host, farm, homestay, or road stop, send one message and check for these 4 answers:

1. Exact Google Maps pin for the actual parking or tenting area.
2. Whether washroom access is available at night, not just during staffed hours.
3. Whether drinking water is available on arrival.
4. The name and number of the person who will pick up if you arrive late.

If even one of these answers is vague, treat it as a meal stop or day stop, not an overnight stop.

## 3) Route / Location Note

The most useful route pattern right now is to stay close to **structured South India corridors** instead of improvising late:

- **Bengaluru -> Chikkamagaluru / Mandya side** has multiple public leads where host-direct contact exists, but overnight proof still needs work.
- **Kerala caravan-park style supply** looks stronger on infrastructure because water, power, and waste-handling are part of the public positioning.

The route lesson: if your trip depends on a late-evening arrival, bias toward places built to receive vehicles, not just places that look camp-friendly in daylight.

## 4) Trust / Host Insight

CampIn should only sound confident when a host can provide the same details we ask for in our host form: exact pin, washroom type, water access, parking fit, photos of the actual stay area, and clear permission status.

If a host says “yes, camping is possible” but cannot show who controls the land or what the overnight setup really looks like, that is still a lead, not a trusted stop.
